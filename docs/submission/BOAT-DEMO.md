# Boat demo and audience feedback

On October 5, 2026, the founder presented a prepared happy-path Fork Night concept at a boat demo night. The founder gathered feedback from the live audience and continued conversations afterward. The account below summarizes the founder's recollection; it is not an interview transcript, and no wording is presented as a verbatim audience quote. Participants remain anonymous.

## What people responded to

The founder recalls interest in:

- Collaborative digital spaces where people can develop a project together.
- Shared inference credit pools for collective experimentation.
- Future support for AI influencer content.
- A GitHub-like home for generative AI content and its history.
- Tracking autonomous bots managing an AI influencer content pipeline.

Conversations afterward included people developing their own AI content creators or AI influencers who expressed interest in the premise. One was with a product manager developing an AI personality as a content creator and considering a broader AI influencer network. Following the history of creative work was a possible application the founder took from that conversation.

These conversations suggest directions for further testing. They do not establish an agreed partnership, adoption, willingness to pay or specific customer requirements.

## How the feedback influenced the work

On October 8, the founder brought a laptop to an AI content creators' event. The founder showed the concept to people working in the space and gathered further feedback. These conversations informed continued exploration; no participant names or verbatim quotes are recorded here.

The central product remains collaborative creation with visible contribution history. The feedback also raised a question: could that history help a person inspect and guide an AI creator's work?

Follow-up work explored storyboard review and a creator companion showing prepared alternatives, selections and human guidance. The implemented companion is a local demonstration with fictional personas. It does not operate an autonomous influencer pipeline or publish to external social platforms.

The shared pilot separately supports real guest contributions, generation and publication. A founder-operated Monad testnet rehearsal subsequently established a working payment-and-claim mechanism. Those implementation results are described in [the project guide](PROJECT.md) and [the onchain proof](ONCHAIN.md).

The next feedback exercise is to have a small group use the hosted pilot and observe where they join, contribute, continue another person's work and return. That would provide stronger evidence than interest expressed during a demonstration.
