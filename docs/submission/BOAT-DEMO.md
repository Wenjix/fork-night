# Boat demo and audience feedback

The founder presented Fork Night at a boat demo night and discussed it with audience members. The account below summarizes the founder's recollection; it is not an interview transcript, and no wording is presented as a verbatim audience quote. Participants remain anonymous.

## What people responded to

The founder recalls interest in:

- Collaborative digital spaces where people can develop a project together.
- Shared inference credit pools for collective experimentation.
- Future support for AI influencer content.
- A GitHub-like home for generative AI content and its history.
- Tracking autonomous bots managing an AI influencer content pipeline.

One conversation was with a product manager developing an AI personality as a content creator and considering a broader AI influencer network. The founder reported that she liked the project. Following the history of creative work was a possible application the founder took from that conversation.

These conversations suggest directions for further testing. They do not establish an agreed partnership, adoption, willingness to pay or specific customer requirements.

## How the feedback influenced the work

The central product remains collaborative creation with visible contribution history. The feedback also raised a question: could that history help a person inspect and guide an AI creator's work?

Follow-up work explored storyboard review and a creator companion showing prepared alternatives, selections and human guidance. The implemented companion is a local demonstration with fictional personas. It does not operate an autonomous influencer pipeline or publish to external social platforms.

The shared pilot separately supports real guest contributions, generation and publication.

The next feedback exercise is to have a small group use the hosted pilot and observe where they join, contribute, continue another person's work and return. That would provide stronger evidence than interest expressed during a demonstration.
